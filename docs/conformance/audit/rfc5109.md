# RFC 5109: RTP Payload Format for Generic Forward Error Correction (ULPFEC)

Spec: RFC 5109, December 2007 (Standards Track; obsoletes RFC 2733 and RFC 3009). Audited at fork master
8916bc3. Sections covered: 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14 (all sections with requirements; 1 to 6, 10, 15 are
descriptive or examples).

**Status of the implementation.** Implemented in `rtc-interceptor/src/ulpfec/` (branch
`conf/cc-fec-ulpfec`): the FEC and level headers, the protection operation with any number of levels,
level-aware recovery, and two carriages as a pair of interceptors, RED (section 14.2, what browsers
negotiate for video) and a separate stream on a repair SSRC. Opt-in: `configure_ulpfec` or the builders;
the default codecs still do not offer `ulpfec` (`tests/default_codecs_omit_ulpfec.rs`), and the application
registers `video/red` and `video/ulpfec` and passes the negotiated payload types. Test names without a
path are in `rtc-interceptor/src/ulpfec/` (unit) or `rtc-interceptor/tests/ulpfec.rs`.

## Gaps, in order of importance

1. **Negotiation drives the interceptors (M).** RFC5109-13-4, 14-1, 14.2-2, 13-1, 14.3-3, 14.3-4: bind
   the RED and FEC payload types and `onelevelonly` per stream from the negotiated codecs (a
   `StreamInfo` field for the RED payload type), tie `ulpfec` to `red` in answers (drop both when the
   FEC payload type is not accepted), check the RED fmtp. Today the application passes the numbers.
2. **Congestion rules (S).** Done: RFC5109-12-1, 12-2 (`FecRate`, `rtc-interceptor/src/cc/fec_rate.rs`:
   the FEC per block follows the receiver-report loss, bounded, and its bit rate comes out of the
   target the application encodes at).
3. **Separate-session FEC (L, low value).** RFC5109-14.1-3, 14.1-5, 14.3-1, 14.3-2, 11-2, and the
   literal SSRC rule of 7.2-2 / 14.1-2 / 14.1-4: FEC in its own RTP session with `a=group:FEC`. No
   WebRTC implementation does this and the engine has one bundled transport. Candidates for a permanent
   "not applicable to WebRTC" decision; the opt-in separate-stream carriage uses a repair SSRC instead.

   What section 14.1 takes, for whoever implements it (nothing of it fits inside the interceptor
   crate alone):
   - **SDP (JSEP).** An `m=application <port> RTP/SAVPF <pt>` section with `a=rtpmap:<pt> ulpfec/<rate>`
     and its own `a=mid`, grouped with the protected section by `a=group:FEC <media-mid> <fec-mid>`
     (RFC 4756; RFC 5888 grouping), never in the `BUNDLE` group: section 14.1 separates the two
     sessions by transport address ("multiplexing [...] is usually provided by the destination
     transport address"). An answerer that does not take it rejects the section (port 0) and drops
     the group (14.3-2); one that takes it answers the same grouping (14.3-1).
   - **Transport.** The FEC section gets its own ICE/DTLS transport and SRTP keys (11-2: "different
     encryption keys for media and FEC when sent in separate streams" follows from separate DTLS
     associations, RFC 5764).
   - **Chains.** The FEC stream carries the protected stream's SSRC (7.2-2, 14.1-2), so it cannot be
     bound in the same interceptor chain as its media: every per-SSRC state there (NACK history, RTCP
     session, congestion control history) would merge the two. The encoder runs on the media
     section's chain (it reads the media wire images, `Slot::FecWireImage`) and hands its FEC
     packets, unnumbered by TWCC, to a second chain bound to the FEC section's transport, where
     they get their own sequence numbers (the FEC stream's, starting at random) and the RTCP of an
     RTP session of their own (SR / RR / SDES under the same SSRC and CNAME). The receiver mirrors
     it: the FEC section's chain hands FEC packets to the decoder on the media chain.
   - **Congestion control.** The two sessions share the path when they share the address pair; the
     FEC rate (`FecRate`) still comes out of the media target.
   Size L. The engine today has one chain per peer connection, so the second-chain hand-off is the
   core of the work.
4. **Tests through a peer connection (M).** RFC5109-11-1: RED + ULPFEC negotiated between two peer
   connections (and against a browser in the interop matrix), FEC packets seen encrypted on the wire.

## Bugs found

None in the new code's own tests. Two things to know:

- The FEC is computed where `Slot::FecEncoder` sits, before the TWCC sender rewrites the transport-cc
  header extension, so with TWCC on the protected image is not the wire image (the same defect as
  FlexFEC draft-03 Bug 1; fixed for both by moving the encoder, tracked with the congestion-control
  work). When the encoder moves, the NACK responder must stay wire-ward of it or see wire sequence
  numbers some other way: in RED carriage with `RedPlacement::OwnPacket` this interceptor renumbers the
  media stream to make room for FEC packets.
- Figures 12 and 15 of the RFC print M recovery as 0 with the four-packet sum of section 10.1 as the
  reason. Over the two packets each of those FEC packets protects at level 0 the operation of 8.1
  gives 1. The encoder follows 8.1; Figure 11 and 14 also print the RTP marker of a FEC packet as 1
  against 7.2 (SHALL be 0), and 0 is sent.
