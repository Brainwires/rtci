# RFC 8108: Sending Multiple RTP Streams in a Single RTP Session: clause audit

Spec: RFC 8108, March 2017 (updates RFC 3550 and RFC 4585). Audited at fork master 8916bc3.

What the engine does. Every sending encoding has its own SSRC (plus one for RTX and one for FlexFEC
when negotiated), and each primary SSRC sends its own SR compound packet on a fixed interval. For
receiving, the engine invents further SSRCs that send no media: one random SSRC per remote stream
for receiver reports (`rtci-interceptor/src/report/receiver_stream.rs:42`), one random SSRC for NACKs
(`nack/generator.rs:139`), one random SSRC for TWCC feedback (`twcc/receiver.rs:164`), and SSRC 0
for the interval PLI generator (`intervalpli/generator.rs:88`) and for RFC 8888 reports by default
(`rfc8888/sender.rs:55`). None of the RTCP timing machinery of RFC 3550 6.3 or RFC 4585 3.5 exists,
so the clauses of section 5.3.2 and 5.4.2, which amend that machinery, are all `missing`.

## Gaps, in order of importance

1. **A coherent set of local SSRCs for RTCP** (RFC8108-5.1-1, 5.4.1-1, 5.4.1-3, 6.1-1, 6.2-1). Size M. Reception
   reports and feedback should come from SSRCs that are participants: the section's sending SSRC when the
   transceiver sends, otherwise one reporting SSRC per media section (or per connection), each with SR/RR,
   CNAME and BYE. Replace the four independent random or zero packet-sender SSRCs with that one choice,
   passed to the interceptors at bind time (`StreamInfo` already travels to every interceptor).
2. **Per-SSRC RTCP scheduling with aggregation** (RFC8108-5-1, 5.2-1 to 5.2-4, 5.3-1, 5.3.1-2, 5.3.2-1 to 5.3.2-6,
   5.4.2-1 to 5.4.2-5). Size L, one work package with RFC 3550 gap 4 and the RFC 4585 early-feedback
   scheduler: a session-level RTCP scheduler that owns tp/tn per SSRC, aggregates what is due into one
   datagram within the MTU, caps the initial burst at four compound packets, and classifies the session
   by counting remote CNAMEs.
3. **Participant timeout** (RFC8108-7.1.4-1, 7.1.4-2). Size S once the scheduler exists: 5 x Td with Tmin 5 s;
   on timeout unbind the remote stream from the interceptors and end the track as a BYE does.
4. **SSRC lifetime** (RFC8108-6.1-2, 6.2-2). Size S. Refuse to revive an SSRC after its BYE has left; pick a new
   SSRC when the clock rate of an encoding changes.

## Bugs found

- **B1. Feedback is sent from SSRCs that are not in the session.** `rtci-interceptor/src/nack/generator.rs:139`
  and `twcc/receiver.rs:164` draw a random packet-sender SSRC; `intervalpli/generator.rs:88` and
  `rfc8888/sender.rs:55` use 0. None is announced in SDP or by SDES, and a random one can equal a remote
  or local media SSRC. Reproduction: receive video with one lost packet and capture RTCP: the RR's sender
  SSRC, the NACK's sender SSRC and the TWCC feedback's sender SSRC are three different values, and only
  the first appears in an SDES. A peer that validates the packet sender against known participants
  (RFC 3550 6.2.1 membership, RFC 4585 6.1) may discard the feedback.
- **B2. Report burst grows with the number of streams.** `report/sender.rs:300` and `report/receiver.rs:249`
  emit one datagram per SSRC on the same tick. Reproduction: bind 3 simulcast layers and 10 remote
  streams, advance one interval: 13 RTCP datagrams leave back to back, every interval, each with its own
  SRTCP overhead, where RFC 8108 5.3 would aggregate them into one or two.

## Since the audit

- **B1 and B2 fixed** by the RTCP session: feedback and reports come from the endpoint's own SSRCs
  (the first media SSRC, or one reporting SSRC with its RR, CNAME and BYE), and all local SSRCs
  report in one compound packet per interval.
- For the owner: 5.1-1 (every SSRC reports on every remote SSRC) is an option, off by default;
  6.1-1 stays violated (the reporting SSRC of a receive-only endpoint says BYE when a sender
  with its own signalled SSRC appears).
